# Secret Words: can we make it? Legality and precedent (4 October 2026)

Product owner, from a research pass of laws and court cases (sources opened 4 October 2026). **This is a summary of
sources, not legal advice.** Before sharing the app beyond family and friends, a short check with a trademark lawyer is
still worth it.

## The answer in one line
**Yes.** The rules of a game are free for anyone to use. What is protected is the **name** "Codenames" (a trademark), and
CGE's **own expression**: word list, rulebook text, artwork, card look. Secret Words uses none of these.

## What the law says
| Question | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Are game rules protected? (US) | No. "The idea for a game is not protected by copyright… the methods for playing it are not protected." The rules' *text* and the art can be | US Copyright Office, Games: https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html ; 17 USC 102(b): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102 |
| Are game rules protected? (India) | No: ideas, themes and methods aren't protected, only their form of expression | R.G. Anand v. Delux Films, Supreme Court 1978: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._G._Anand_v._Deluxe_Films |
| Can we mention their trademark? (US) | Yes, if needed to identify the product, only as much as needed (no logo or styling), and nothing suggests endorsement. A disclaimer helps | New Kids on the Block v. News America (1992): https://openjurist.org/971/f2d/302/ ; Toyota v. Tabari (2010): https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1530600.html |
| Can we mention their trademark? (India) | Yes, use to identify the owner's goods is allowed when it follows "honest practices" and takes no unfair advantage | Trade Marks Act 1999, s.30: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1881111/ |
| (EU, for completeness) | Referring to a mark is honest if it doesn't suggest a connection or present the product as a copy | Gillette v. LA-Laboratories, CJEU 2005 |

## Precedent: what happened to others
| Case | What happened | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Scrabble v. Scrabulous**, Delhi High Court 2008 | No copyright in Scrabble's game or rules; but the name "Scrabulous" was too close to "Scrabble" and was stopped. Relaunched as **Lexulous**; Hasbro withdrew the case | **The closest precedent, and in India:** same game is fine, a look-alike name is not. https://spicyip.com/2008/09/scrabble-vs-scrabulous-reports-on-delhi.html |
| DaVinci v. ZiKo (Bang! clone), US 2016 | Same mechanics, different theme and art: **no infringement** | A new theme and look protects us. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/texas-court-affirms-game-mechanics-not-protected-under-copyright-law |
| Tetris v. Xio (Mino), US 2012 | Copied the exact look and feel: **infringed** | Never copy their look |
| Spry Fox v. 6waves (Triple Town), US 2012 | A reskin made after seeing the game under NDA: case allowed to proceed, settled | Same |
| Wordle (NYT), 2024 | NYT took down ~1,900 GitHub copies using the Wordle name and look; said it has "no issue" with similar games that don't | Name and look are what owners act on |
| **Codenames clone**, 2020 | CGE politely asked playcodenamesonline.com (Codenames in its web address) to take down the game and its GitHub code; the maker did. Many clones with their own names still run (e.g. horsepaste.com since about 2016) | CGE's practice so far: a polite request, not a lawsuit, and aimed at a site using its name |

CGE owns the US trademark CODENAMES (Reg. No. 5133198, 2017). An Indian or EU registration couldn't be confirmed either way.

## How Secret Words measures up
| Element | Ours | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Rules (5 × 5 board, one word + number, guesses, the losing card) | Same as the convention | ✅ not protected |
| Name | Secret Words | ✅ not close to "Codenames". **Avoid "Code Words", "Codenamez" and similar** |
| Words | Our own 452 India-centric words | ✅ |
| Look | Orange v teal teams with funny names, our shape icons, Tambola's clean style | ✅ not red/blue agents, beige cards or spy art |
| Theme and terms | Clue giver, the map, the Landmine | ✅ no spymaster, agent, assassin, key card |
| Rules text | "How to play" written in our own words | ✅ never copy CGE's rulebook |
| "Inspired by Codenames" | Plain text, with a not-endorsed line | ✅ referential use; the residual risk is a polite request to remove the line, a one-line fix |

## The credit line (decided, K22)
Plain text, no logo, no special styling, never in a heading, button, page title, web address or the repo's name:
- **Game card:** "Inspired by Codenames · team word hunt · 4–20 players · about 15 min a game"
- **How to play (bottom):** "Secret Words uses game rules inspired by Codenames, designed by Vlaada Chvátil. Codenames is a
  trademark of Czech Games Edition. Secret Words is an independent free game and is not made, sponsored or endorsed by
  Czech Games Edition."

## Remaining risks, and what we'd do
| Risk | Likelihood | If it happens |
|---|---|---|
| CGE asks us to remove "Inspired by Codenames" | Low | Remove the two lines (C1 change, minutes) |
| CGE asks us to take the game down although the name differs | Low (their 2020 request was to a site using their name) | Talk to a lawyer; the cases above favour us |
| A copyright takedown on the GitHub repo | Very low: it needs copied expression, and we copy none | Counter-notice, or remove the item named |
| An app-store complaint (if we ever publish there) | Low | Same name rules apply there |
