# Word game "in the style of Codenames": a short guide (working name "Word Link", draft, 4 October 2026)

Our own game: our own name, words, roles and look. Game mechanics are free to use; the name "Codenames", its artwork,
look, rulebook text and word list belong to Czech Games Edition and are never copied (sources below).

## Overview
Two teams race to find their own secret words on a shared grid of words. One player per team, the **clue-giver**,
knows which words belong to whom. Each turn the clue-giver says **one word and a number** ("Cricket, 3") linking as
many of their team's words as they dare; teammates then tap words one at a time. Find all your words first and win;
touch the one **danger word** and lose.
- **Players:** 4 to 12 in two teams (uneven teams are fine); 2–3 players can play together against the phone.
- **Time:** about 15 minutes a game. **Ages:** 10+ as clue-giver, 8+ as guessers (Easy board).

## What you need
Without the app: a box of word cards, a key card, team tiles, a sand timer. **With the app: one phone** (a second
phone for the clue-givers is better, see "How we play it").

## How to play
1. Split into two teams; each picks a clue-giver. The two clue-givers sit together.
2. The phone deals a grid of words and secretly marks each word: team A's, team B's, nobody's, or the danger word.
   The team that starts has one extra word.
3. Only the clue-givers see the secret key.
4. The starting team's clue-giver says one word and a number, e.g. "Monsoon, 2".
5. Their teammates discuss and tap words one at a time (up to the number plus 1). Their own word: keep going. Nobody's
   word or the other team's: turn over. The danger word: game over, the other team wins.
6. Teams alternate. The first team to find all its words wins.

## How Pocket Game Night plays it
- **Phone 1 (the board):** lies in the middle, face up, showing the grid in big type; guessers tap a word, confirm,
  and it turns to its team's colour **and pattern** (never colour alone).
- **Key for the clue-givers, two ways:**
  - **Two phones (recommended):** phone 1 shows a short code; the clue-givers open it on a second phone, which shows
    the key only (works offline: the same code makes the same board). The clue-givers keep that phone between them.
  - **One phone:** before each clue, "Pass to the clue-giver", who holds to peek at the key (the same private
    hold-to-see as Impostor), lets go, gives the clue aloud, and puts the phone back in the middle.
- **Spoken clues:** the clue is said aloud; the phone only shows whose turn it is, the number chosen ("Clue: 3"), the
  guesses left, and each team's words left.
- **Words:** our own India-centric list, fair across regions and ages, with words that have two meanings (DUCK,
  PITCH, TIFFIN, SIGNAL, POWER) because those make the best boards (`words.md`, to come).

## What we do differently
No offline one-phone version with Indian words exists: today's versions need everyone online, a TV, or the box, and
the Indian apps are full of paywalls and sign-in walls. Ours: offline, one or two phones, no sign-in, free, words fair
for a Chennai–Delhi–Calicut–Kolkata table and for kids and elders, an Easy board for families, and the evening's
scores across games.
