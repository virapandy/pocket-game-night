# What Am I?: a short guide (draft, 22 October 2026)

Step 1 of `docs/new-game-process.md`. Fit check and player stories: `fit-and-stories.md`. Open rule questions for the
owner: `questions-2026-10-22.md` (WA1–WA10); rows below marked "WA…" follow the recommendation until the owner
answers. Once scenarios exist, `scenarios.md` wins wherever it differs from this file.

## Overview
One player, the **guesser**, holds the phone to their forehead with the screen facing the room. The phone shows a
word. Everyone else gives clues: talk, act, make the noise, hum the tune, but never say the word. The guesser tilts the
phone down when they get it and up to pass. After 60 seconds the phone shows the room what was got and what was
passed, and the phone goes to the next player.

- **Other names:** Heads Up! (the phone app, Warner Bros), Hedbanz (the box, yes/no questions), "Who Am I?" (sticky
  notes on foreheads). Close cousins: Catch Phrase, Time's Up / Monikers. Ours is a folk game with our own name, words
  and look; we never use the Heads Up! name, logo or deck names.
- **Players:** 2 to 20; best with 4 to 10. **Time:** about 1½ minutes a turn; a round (everyone once) with 6 players
  is about 10 minutes; an evening is 2 to 4 rounds.
- **Suits:** ages about 6 and up as clue givers (acting needs no reading), about 8 and up as guessers, up to grandparents.

## What you need
Without the app: someone who writes words on slips or sticky notes (and so sees them all and sits out), a timer, and
paper for the score. The box (Hedbanz) has 69 picture cards, 6 headbands, chips and a sand timer. **With the app: one
phone.** Nobody sits out, nobody has seen the words, every turn is the same length, the score is kept.

## How to play
1. Type the names (or keep tonight's). Pick a word pack (WA8).
2. The phone says "Riya, you're guessing" and shows how to hold it: **sideways, screen to the room, on your forehead or
   chest**. Or, **hands-free**: stand the phone behind the guesser and one clue giver taps for them (owner, 23 October).
3. "3, 2, 1": the first word appears big for the room.
4. The room gives clues. The guesser **tilts down for Got it** or **up for Pass** (or taps the big buttons, WA2). Each
   mark plays a sound and a buzz, because the guesser can't see the screen. The next word appears at once.
5. A chime at 10 seconds left; the turn ends at 60 seconds (WA1). A word on screen when time runs out counts as passed.
6. **End of turn:** the room sees the list: got words, passed words. A word marked by mistake can be tapped to swap it
   before "Keep score" (WA4).
7. "Pass the phone to Arjun": the next player guesses. When everyone has guessed once, that is a round; the scoreboard
   shows tonight's totals (WA7).
8. "End game" shows tonight's winner and the funniest misses; "Play again" keeps the people, new words.

## Words
Packs are the game. Every word passes the "known everywhere" rule (K24: English, or an Indian word a non-Hindi speaker
from Tirunelveli or Sivagangai knows), can be acted or described by a 10-year-old, and is checked by the Jev word-list
check before approval. Avoid Hindi-film-only and Hindi-song decks: they shut out much of the south [research, I];
regional films can come later as opt-in packs. Real people only if known in every state and not political. Proposed
first packs in WA8; the word lists come after the owner's answers, as `words.csv` (IDs WAIW-).

## How Pocket Game Night plays it
- **The phone does:** deals words nobody has seen, the timer and chime, the marks (tilt and tap), sounds and buzz for
  the guesser, the end-of-turn list with fixes, the score, whose turn is next, no repeats tonight.
- **The room does:** the clues, the acting, the shouting, judging whether a clue broke the rules.
- **Sideways screen:** the word fills the screen in landscape, readable from 3 metres. Every other screen follows the
  usual portrait layout.
- **Tilt on iPhone:** the browser asks permission for motion before the first turn ("Allow tilt"); if refused or not
  available, tap buttons only, without fuss [K].
- **Sound and buzz:** on by default during a turn (the guesser can't see); Android buzzes, iPhone browsers can't, so
  sound carries it there.

## Variants (not in the first release)
- **Teams:** two teams, turns alternate, team totals (WA3).
- **Act it out only:** a silent round, as some groups play [C].
- **Three rounds, one deck** (Time's Up / Monikers): any clue, then one word only, then mime only, with the same 40
  words [G].
- **Pictures for small children and non-readers** (Heads Up! Kids uses pictures [E]): needs our own drawings; later.
- **Hedbanz style:** the guesser asks yes/no questions; slower; owner chose Heads Up style (22 October).

## What real players say (research, 22 October 2026)
- Fast and loud, a family-night staple [A]; the acting keeps people from just shouting hints [I].
- **Paid decks** and lost purchases are the top complaint [A, B]: ours are all free.
- **Words kids don't know** ("bagpipes", "medusa") spoil turns [Madison library review, snippet only]; packs must be
  everyday.
- **Recording** the clue givers worries parents [D]: we never record.
- **iPhone motion** needs permission and HTTPS in the browser [K]: tap fallback is required.
- No sourced complaints found about tilt misfires, phones falling or repeated words; we design for them anyway (fix
  at end of turn, no repeats). Reddit and BoardGameGeek blocked automated reading.
