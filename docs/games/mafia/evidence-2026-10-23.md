# Mafia: what each decision rests on (product owner, 23 October 2026)

Owner, 23 October: "hope your decisions are based on actual human game play research and patterns by other products and not
your call only". This is the honest audit of MF1–MF17 and the design calls in the screen map.

**What research reached** (two passes, 22 and 23 October): Wikipedia (Mafia; Werewolf), Game On Family's how-to, a review of
One Night Ultimate Werewolf, a Werewolf review (Board's Eye View), app store listings (Mafia Moderator, Wolvesville, Bezier's
moderator app, an open-source pass-and-play Werewolf app), Indian event listings (IRL Mafia in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai,
Pune), Raja Mantri Chor Sipahi, a forum game report, a maths paper on balance, a discussion of no-vote days.
**What it did not reach:** BoardGameGeek (blocked to automated reading, and a bot check in the browser), Reddit (blocked),
Bezier's rule book (rate-limited), app review text (only star ratings). So **players' own reports of real games are the
missing evidence**, and nothing below should be read as resting on them.

Strength: **strong** = the convention in several sources · **some** = one source, or our own released game · **ours** =
the product owner's reasoning, no outside evidence.

## What this means
1. **MF16 should not have been recommended on a simulation alone.** Our own process says conventions win by default
   (`docs/new-game-process.md`, step 1), and the human-play sources lean towards *more* Mafia, not fewer. The simulation's
   random-voting Village is weaker than real families. **Owner, 23 October: back to the convention table (MF5); MF16 and MF17 dropped.** The
   simulation is a question for real play, not an answer. MF17 likewise: keep 5 players as the convention (1 Mafia, 4
   Villagers) until evidence says otherwise.
2. **MF1 is the biggest bet and has no outside evidence.** It is also the game's main idea. Before scenarios, it should be
   proven on the prototype (six persona plays, Jev's "could a watcher tell the role?") and then by the AI release-candidate
   playthrough; families' first games and their "Report a problem" notes are the real test.
3. **Ways to get real players' evidence** without hand testing: (a) the owner, or anyone with a normal browser, opens a
   few BoardGameGeek threads that our tools can't (the product owner can list them), and pastes the text in; (b) app review
   text from the Play Store on a phone; (c) a one-question message to family groups who have played Mafia ("how many Mafia
   for how many people, and what made it boring?").
