# Mafia: balance check of our role counts (product owner, 23 October 2026)

**Why.** The second research pass (23 October) found no published win rates for 6–16 players with a Doctor and Police; the
only advice is rules of thumb (about 1 Mafia in 3 to 1 in 4; a square root for big groups, from a maths paper). So the
product owner ran a rough simulation: 20,000 games per player count, our rules (night first, Doctor once on themselves and
never the same player twice running, Police alignment only, role shown when voted out, Mafia win when equal to the rest).

**Two kinds of play, as the bounds** (real families sit between them; Wikipedia notes the Village wins more often in
live play than theory predicts):
- *Random votes:* the Village votes at random. The worst a Village can do.
- *Police shares:* the Police tells the room what they found while still in, and the Village votes a found Mafia, or
  otherwise someone not cleared. A Village that plays well.
Mafia pick at random among the Village; the Doctor protects at random. A crude model: it shows where the counts are out of
line with each other, not the exact chance of winning.

## What it says
1. **Each step up in Mafia comes one player too early**: the Village's chance halves at 8, 12 and 16 players. Moving each
   step one player later (1 Mafia up to 8, 2 up to 12, 3 up to 16) removes the three worst rows and never lets the Village
   win more than about 3 in 4 even when it plays well.
2. **5 players is the weakest game** (the Village wins 1 in 4 whatever it does, because with no Doctor or Police it has no
   information). Adding a Doctor at 5 helps a little (1 in 3).
3. **Days with nobody out help the Mafia** (a source argues a random vote on day 1 is better for the Village than no vote).
   "Nobody today" (MF12) and the second tie (MF6) stay as decided; How to play could say "Skipping a vote helps the Mafia."
